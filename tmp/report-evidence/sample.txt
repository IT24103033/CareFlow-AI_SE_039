1. Project Overview and Scope
1.1 Business problem
Emergency and urgent-care pathways lose critical minutes between the moment a patient reports symptoms and the moment a clinician decides what should happen next. Symptom reports sit in unstructured channels, scheduling information is held separately from clinical urgency, and field nurses are dispatched by phone without a recorded safety check. CarePulse is an emergency healthcare triage and field-response platform that closes this gap: a patient reports symptoms on a mobile phone, an agentic AI workflow structures and scores the case, a doctor reviews and approves high-risk cases on a web dashboard, and an approved case is dispatched to an available field nurse whose GPS progress and on-site vitals are recorded and shown back to the patient.
1.2 Objectives
Reduce the delay between symptom report and a clinically accountable decision by structuring the case with a four-agent workflow.
Guarantee that no emergency dispatch executes without an explicit, recorded, authorised doctor approval (human-in-the-loop safety gate).
Give every actor a role-appropriate interface on a single shared backend, database, identity and rule set (React for staff, Flutter for patients and field nurses).
Keep a complete audit trail: patient record changes, AI plans, workflow events, approvals, assignments, GPS route logs and vitals.

1.5 Honest scope boundaries
The repository’s own handover documents (docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md, docs/DEVELOPER_GUIDE.md) state several deliberate limits, which this report repeats rather than hides:
The ETA used in dispatch is a straight-line (Haversine × 1.4) estimate; GoogleMapsService does not call a live routing API. Patient-contact SMS is simulated (SimulatedSmsNotificationService).
Regular-expression minimisation of e-mails and long identifiers is applied before text is sent to Gemini; it is not full anonymisation of free text. Synthetic data must be used for evaluation.
Location tracking is foreground-only. Failed vitals drafts are kept for 24 hours per account and case; this is not a general offline-sync engine.
No clinical-production-readiness claim is made. The AI output is a decision-support input; a doctor remains accountable.

4. Agentic AI Architecture
The agentic subsystem solves a multi-step domain problem – turning a free-text symptom report into a validated, auditable, human-approved dispatch decision – and is not a chatbot or single-prompt generator. TriageWorkflowRunner orchestrates four agents with different responsibilities, contracts and tool permissions.

Figure 6. Agentic AI architecture.
4.1 The four distinct agents

Each agent counts as distinct under §9.1: it has an identifiable responsibility, its own input/output contract, different tool permissions and a visible entry in the persisted workflow record.
4.2 Orchestration, planning and delegation
POST /api/v1/triage/submit authenticates the caller and verifies patient ownership (RequirePatientAsync).
Planner creates and persists an AiWorkflow row (ExecutionStatus = Running) with the objective, context snapshot, tool-call summary, model name and the validated three-step plan.
Domain analysis creates the TriageTicket, RiskAssessment, AiTriageLog and, for HIGH, the ApprovalQueue entry; the ticket id is linked to the workflow and saved *before* any further external call.
The Action agent runs the typed search_available_slots tool for the recommended specialty; the result and duration are appended as an event.
The Validation agent evaluates safety. If the workflow or assessment failed, the ticket is forced to NEEDS_DOCTOR_APPROVAL; the final status is AwaitingApproval, ManualReviewRequired or Completed.
Doctor approval/rejection and later assignment, arrival, escalation and completion each append a workflow event atomically with the business change (WorkflowProgress.RecordAsync).
4.3 Shared state and observability
Workflow state is durable in PostgreSQL (AiWorkflow): workflow id, objective, patient context snapshot, plan (StepsJson), tool-call summary, ExecutionStatus, ExecutionJson (ordered events with step, timestamp, structured result and duration), model used, error message, review fields and FinishedAt. Hidden chain-of-thought, passwords and tokens are not stored. GET /api/v1/triage/{id} returns the structured execution summary to authorised users, and the React doctor modal and Flutter status stepper render it.
4.4 Human approval
The defined high-impact action is *dispatching a nurse to a patient*. A HIGH-risk or failed case pauses at NEEDS_DOCTOR_APPROVAL. Only the Doctor role can call approve/reject (Admin cannot), a Running workflow cannot be decided, and assignment requires that the approval record exists, was made by a still-active doctor, and that the caller is that doctor or an Admin. Safety warnings additionally need an explicit AcknowledgeSafetyFlags.

6. API Design
All routes are versioned under /api/v1, use JSON, nouns and correct verbs, and return conventional status codes: 200/201 success, 204 delete, 400 validation (FluentValidation / ApiProblem), 401 unauthenticated/expired, 403 forbidden/ownership, 404 not found, 409 conflict/state or concurrency, 502 provider error, 503 safe workflow failure. A fallback authorisation policy requires an authenticated user on every endpoint except /auth/* and /health. Swagger/OpenAPI with a Bearer security scheme is generated by Swashbuckle (enabled in Development or with Swagger:Enabled=true).
Error body (all failures): { "statusCode": 409, "message": "…", "traceId": "…" }. Collections use page/pageSize with an upper bound (dispatch: 1–100). Operations are asynchronous end-to-end (async/await, EF …Async).

9. Technical Report
9.1 Implementation highlights
Atomic, race-safe dispatch. AssignDispatch re-reads triage and nurse after the external safety call, then writes the dispatch, nurse claim, ticket status and workflow event in one SaveChanges; the partial unique index and xmin make a simultaneous second claim fail with 409 while the case stays in the waiting queue (ConcurrentAssignments_ClaimNurseOnce…).
Concurrency-safe booking. AppointmentSlot.RowVersion ensures exactly one of two simultaneous bookings succeeds (TwoSimultaneousBookingAttempts_OnlyOneSucceeds); past slots are rejected server-side.
Audit by construction. CarePulseDbContext.SaveChangesAsync builds field-level PatientAuditLog entries (old/new values) for patient entities and stamps timestamps and soft-delete metadata.
Identity derivation. Consultation patient/doctor come from the booked slot; dispatch doctor comes from the approval record; nurse operations resolve through OwnTicket – the client cannot supply or substitute these ids.
Timezones. Storage in UTC; roster and AI date filters in Asia/Colombo (UTC+05:30) via ClinicTime.
